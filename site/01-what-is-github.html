1. What is GitHub?

GitHub is a website where people keep their projects and work on them together. Think of it like Google Docs, but for anything you can save as a file: writing, code, spreadsheets, lists, plans, and more.

Every project lives in its own repository — a folder with a history of every change ever made. You can see who changed what, when, and why.

You do not have to be a coder to use GitHub. People use it for cookbooks, novels, class notes, town newsletters, family recipes, and anything else they want to keep track of and share.

The big idea in three steps

  1. Someone starts a project and puts it on GitHub.
  2. Other people copy it, make changes, and ask to share those changes back.
  3. The project owner decides what to keep.

That last step happens through a pull request, which we cover later.

Your GitHub profile

When you sign up, you get a profile page. It shows the projects you own, the projects you contribute to, and your activity. You can fill in as much or as little about yourself as you like.

Nothing in this guide asks you to share your address, phone number, or any private information. You stay in control of what you share.

Public versus private

  • Public repositories can be seen by anyone. Most beginner projects are

public, and that is what this guide uses.

  • Private repositories are only visible to people you invite.

Public projects are the easiest to practice with, so we will stick with those.
