# 1. What is GitHub?

GitHub is a website where people keep their projects and work on them
together. Think of it like Google Docs, but for anything you can save as a
file: writing, code, spreadsheets, lists, plans.

Every project lives in its own **repository** — a folder with a history of
every change ever made. You can see who changed what, when, and why.

You do not have to be a coder to use GitHub. People use it for cookbooks,
novels, class notes, town newsletters, and more.

---

## The big idea in three steps

1. Someone starts a project and puts it on GitHub.
2. Other people copy it, make changes, and ask to share those changes back.
3. The project owner decides what to keep.

That last step happens through a **pull request**, which we cover later.

---

## Your GitHub profile

When you sign up, you get a profile page. It shows the projects you own, the
projects you contribute to, and your activity. You can fill in as much or as
little about yourself as you like. Nothing in this guide asks you to share
your address, phone number, or any private information.

---

## Public versus private

- **Public repositories** can be seen by anyone. Most beginner projects are
  public.
- **Private repositories** are only visible to people you invite.

This guide uses public examples because they are the easiest to practice
with.
